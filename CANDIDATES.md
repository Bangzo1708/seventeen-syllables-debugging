# Candidate haiku — Seventeen Syllables of Debugging

All three candidates are original, written by hand for this bounty
(no copying from the internet or from any example). Each is a strict
5-7-5 haiku about debugging, judged against the published criteria in
order: (1) 5-7-5 form about debugging, (2) concrete, fresh centre image,
(3) the turn between line 2 and line 3, (4) every word earns its place,
(5) reads aloud like speech. Shorter text wins ties, so brevity counted.

## Candidate A — CHOSEN

```
Cursor blinks at three
the log repeats one red line
the typo was mine
```

- Syllables: 5-7-5. Line 1: Cur-sor(2) blinks(1) at(1) three(1).
  Line 2: the(1) log(1) re-peats(2) one(1) red(1) line(1).
  Line 3: the(1) ty-po(2) was(1) mine(1).
- Total: 70 characters including newlines.
- Why it won: the centre image is the most concrete and alive of the
  three — a cursor blinking at 3 a.m. and a log that *repeats* one red
  line (a loop you can watch, not a vague statement about code). The
  turn lands hardest: two lines of machine behaviour, then the blame
  snaps back to the human — and not to a grand "bug" but to one small
  *typo*, which is specific, funny, and true to how debugging actually
  ends. No filler: "one red line" is doing real work (the same error,
  again), and every word is needed for the count. Reads aloud cleanly,
  like someone telling you what happened at 3 a.m.

## Candidate B

```
The stack trace ends
where the last good build once stood
now only silence
```

- Syllables: 5-7-5. Line 1: The(1) stack(1) trace(1) ends(1).
  Line 2: where(1) the(1) last(1) good(1) build(1) once(1) stood(1).
  Line 3: now(1) on-ly(2) si-lence(2).
- Total: 73 characters including newlines.
- Why it lost: quiet and bleak, and the turn works ("where the build
  once stood" → "now only silence"), but the centre image is less
  concrete than A's — a stack trace *ending* is an absence, not a
  picture you can see, and "once stood" leans on a slightly worn
  ruin-metaphor. It is the most literary of the three, but the least
  *debugging* — it could be about any vanished thing. Also 3
  characters longer than A, which matters on the tie-break.

## Candidate C

```
Three a.m. cursor
blinks at one red line of code
the bug was mine
```

- Syllables: 5-7-5. Line 1: Three(1) a.m.(2) cur-sor(2).
  Line 2: blinks(1) at(1) one(1) red(1) line(1) of(1) code(1).
  Line 3: the(1) bug(1) was(1) mine(1).
- Total: 68 characters including newlines (shortest of the three).
- Why it lost: the shortest, and the self-blame turn is there, but
  "of code" in line 2 is filler added only to reach seven syllables —
  it fails criterion 4 outright. "The bug was mine" is also vaguer
  than A's "the typo was mine": a bug could be anything, a typo is
  the exact small human mistake. Two characters shorter does not make
  up for a filler phrase and a weaker turn.

## Decision

Candidate A. It is the only one of the three that is strong on all
five criteria at once: strict form, the freshest concrete image, the
sharpest turn, zero filler, and a natural spoken rhythm — and at 70
characters it is nearly as short as the shortest candidate.
